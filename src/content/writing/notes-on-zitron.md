Ed Zitron probably shaped what I do on this site, in some small way. I found Brian Merchant and Cory Doctorow through Better Offline, I found Permacomputing through Blood in the Machine, and this whole nexus of thoughts about tech led me to trying to learn how to create sites and think out loud on line. So, it is with respect and careful thought that I say, I fear he's gone off the deep end.

I really, really understand what drives him. I was, and am, extremely open to his furious arguments against the corrupt, unethical expansion of the AI industry, and the collapse of tech journalism in its face. But I stopped reading him a while ago; the genuine anger in post after post, week after week, became quite off-putting, because I couldn't help but feel it could not be healthy for him to be channelling so much rage. And more recently, I realised my other concern, which is that I no longer trust that if the evidence did prove him wrong, that he would accept it.

I do still largely believe he's right: that the AI industry as currently structured is a titanic bubble, that the debts racked up for the buildout cannot reasonably be balanced by revenues in the real world. But he's started to exhibit the tell-tale pattern of doomsday prophets - the apocalypse keeps getting delayed. The bubble is always 6 months from bursting, and I haven't seen much reflection from him about why his predictions have been so consistently off base. Also, the more I expand the sources I listen to for financial news, the more aware I become that this stuff is complicated, and Ed runs a PR agency, and is basically self-taught in this stuff.

This is not conclusive. I want to engage with his more recent writing a bit more critical, and seek some critical views as well, and form a better evidenced opinion.
